##D4. Summary of key findings

=== Cell 65 ===
The data covers the period from January 1 to May 9, 2025 in New York, and looks at how temperature and light intensity relate to emotional (sentiment) scores a core question in understanding Seasonal Affective Disorder.

**1. Warmer weather links to better mood.**

  When the emotional score was positive (above 0), the average temperature was around 9.96°C. When the emotional score was negative (below 0), the average temperature dropped to just 2.99°C. That's a difference suggesting that colder conditions tend to align with lower emotional states.

**2. Overall mood leaned slightly negative across the study period.**

The average emotional score from January to May 2025 was -0.011, sitting just below neutral. This is consistent with SAD patterns, where the start of the year in New York which is the coldest months tends to suppress emotional wellbeing before gradually recovering into spring.

**3. Both light and temperature rise as sentiment improves.**

The charts show that shortwave radiation (light intensity) and temperature both trend upward from January through May. Sentiment scores follow a similar direction, becoming less volatile and more consistently positive by April–May. This points to a seasonal recovery pattern that mirrors more daylight and warmth.

##D5 Further research

=== Cell 67 ===
**1. Lag effect analysis**
It needs further testing for time-lag correlation between temperature/light and sentiment to reveal whether the body needs time to adjust. Does mood improve immediately with better weather, or is there a delay of a few days or weeks?

**2. Extending the dataset**
The study ends in early May. Tracking through summer and back into winter would allow a full seasonal cycle analysis and stronger conclusions about the SAD pattern.

**3. Negative sentiment outliers**
There are sharp dips in sentiment even during warmer months (visible in May). Understanding what causes these short-term mood drops could reveal that SAD isn't purely environmental.
